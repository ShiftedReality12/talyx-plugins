# Brief structure

Ten sections, in this order. Target length: readable in five minutes. Every fact carries a source reference to the source log in section 10.

## 1. Header

Name, title, organisation, meeting date, meeting type (prospect, referral source, partner, relationship), meeting objective, research status (`FULL` or `LIMITED`), and any intake gaps.

## 2. Snapshot

Four short cards:

- **Who they are**: current role, career path, credentials. Three to five lines.
- **Their organisation now**: what it does in its own words, and what changed recently.
- **In their words**: two or three direct quotes with sources, or "Themes from their public presence" if there are none.
- **Recent developments**: news worth acknowledging at the start of the meeting. If none, say so and plan to ask.

## 3. Strategic read

Three to five sentences. Why this meeting matters to them, not to you. What they are likely working on, what pressure they are under, and where the user's work connects. Written in the person's own domain language.

## 4. Opening

Two or three openers, ordered by relationship:

- Long personal relationship: personal first, then professional milestones.
- Professional relationship: shared context first, then a recent development.
- New introduction: respect for their work first, then a light rapport question.

Each opener is one or two sentences and names something specific from research.

## 5. Discovery questions

Three to five questions. At least one is written for this person alone and names something specific from research. Each question is one question, 50 words or fewer, followed by one line: "What the answer tells you."

## 6. Their situation, framed

Three steps, 120 words or fewer, in their language:

1. **Gap**: the distance between where their organisation is and where it needs to be.
2. **Bridge**: how the user's work closes that gap, stated as an outcome, not a method.
3. **Urgency**: what changes for them if the gap stays open, tied to something specific in their market.

## 7. Value and next step

- One thing of value the user can offer in the meeting (an introduction, a relevant piece of research, a useful observation).
- The ask: one clear next step that fits the meeting type.
- A fallback ask if the first one does not land.

## 8. Guardrails

- Communication style and what it means for pace and language.
- Talk time: listen more than you speak. If you have been talking for 60 seconds, stop and ask a question.
- Things to confirm in the meeting (facts marked `[UNVERIFIED]`).
- Topics left out on purpose, and why.
- Never ask: "Does that make sense?", "Is this helpful?", "No pressure", "You'd know better than me."

## 9. Follow-up note draft

A short note to send after the meeting, with one placeholder for something said in the meeting and one reference to a specific fact from research. Include a two-line text-message version.

## 10. Source log

Numbered list: fact, URL, `[DIRECT]` or `[SEARCH]`, date accessed.
